What the number means

Clicks per second is just clicks divided by seconds, which makes it easy to compare and easy to misread. Two things move it more than skill does: the length of the test, because nobody sustains a one-second burst for a minute, and the input device, because two thumbs on a touchscreen alternate in a way one finger on a mouse button cannot. Compare like with like or the comparison means nothing.

Rough bands for a 5-second test with a mouse, which is the common default:

CPSWhere that sits
under 4Unhurried, or a trackpad. Perfectly normal for ordinary use.
4 – 6Comfortable steady clicking, one finger.
6 – 8The middle of the distribution. Most people who try hard land here.
8 – 10Fast. Usually a light-switch mouse and a practised finger.
10 – 14Almost always a technique — jitter or butterfly clicking, not ordinary clicks.
over 14Drag clicking, a mouse with a faulty or double-firing switch, or software.

These are approximate, and anyone quoting an exact global average is inventing it — there is no register of everybody’s clicks. Treat them as a sense of scale, not a scoreboard.

Clicking faster, and the part nobody mentions

The techniques people use to push past ordinary clicking are real: jitter clicking tenses the forearm so the tremor drives the button, butterfly clicking alternates two fingers on one button, and drag clicking pulls a fingertip across the button so friction triggers it many times.

They also all work by doing something repetitive and slightly violent to a hand and forearm you would like to keep. Jitter clicking in particular is deliberate sustained tension in exactly the tissue that repetitive strain injury affects. It is worth knowing that before chasing a number: a personal best on a click test is not worth a wrist that hurts in six months, and that trade catches people who are convinced it will not.

A word about games

Plenty of people arrive at a click test because of a game. Worth saying plainly: many online games forbid automation and will suspend an account for it, and that is true of every auto clicker, not just this one. Read the rules of the game you are playing and make your own decision. Idle games, single-player games and ordinary desktop drudgery are a different question entirely.

Questions

Does clicking here send anything anywhere?

No. The test is a few dozen lines of JavaScript in this page. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and there are no third-party scripts on this page at all. Your best score is kept in your own browser’s local storage and never leaves it.

Why does my best score not survive?

It is stored per browser and per device, in local storage. A private window, a different browser, or clearing site data all start it fresh.

Can I use the keyboard?

Yes — once the box has focus, Space and Enter count as clicks. That measures something slightly different, since a keyboard repeats differently from a mouse button, but the test does not care.

Is 10 seconds or 100 seconds the “real” test?

Neither is more real. Short tests measure peak speed, long tests measure endurance, and they answer different questions. The one thing that matters is comparing scores at the same length.
